# Freezing and editions

An **edition** is a canonical version: the text the organisation stands behind.

- **Freezing** stops editing while something outside the document is resolved — an approval that takes time, a discussion with whoever approves. A document need not be frozen to be made canonical. *Freeze* and *Unfreeze* are one toggle on the latest accepted version of the document's path. A document can be frozen only while nobody holds it. Everyone who reads the document sees that it is frozen; who froze it is shown to holders of a history right and to administrators.
- **A freeze is not an edition.** Nothing about the text changes: every version stays as it was made. While frozen, only someone who may accept can edit, and their acceptances continue the numbering.
- ***Make canonical as N.0…***, on the latest accepted version of the path, makes it the next edition, frozen or not, while nobody holds the document; its window names the version, the edition it becomes and that this cannot be undone, and asks before it acts. A freeze open then ends with it. It is refused while any version since the latest acceptance waits for review.
- **How a freeze ends.** By making the document canonical, or by *Unfreeze*, which a holder of the right to freeze and unfreeze chooses once nobody holds the document; editing then resumes at the same accepted number. Where nobody may do either, an administrator asks the platform's operator, who ends it.
- **Edition numbers** are **N.0**: the first edition is **1.0**, the next **2.0**. After edition 1.0 the next acceptance is **1.1**, and the first version waiting for review is **1.0-1**.
- **An edition is never undone.** A mistake is corrected in the text, accepted and made canonical as the next edition; the history keeps both.
- **Who reads what.** A reader who may see only canonical text reads the latest edition, and does not see a document until its first edition exists.

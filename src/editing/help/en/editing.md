# Editing

*Edit*, on the latest version of the document's path, opens the whole document in the editor at once. While you edit, the document is held for you, and your draft is a point of its own on the path, numbered as the version it will become; its acts stand beneath it.

The editor has two views of the same text: the formatted view and the source view, which shows the text as Markdown with each paragraph on one line. Switch between them with the control above the text.

The toolbar starts with *Undo*, *Redo* and *Clear formatting*. *Clear formatting* acts on the selection, or on the line the cursor stands in when nothing is selected: bold, italic, underline and links go, the lines leave lists, quotes and callouts, and headings become paragraphs — the same words, plain. The roles, actions and terms you marked, and mentions of other documents, stay; a table keeps its shape. One *Undo* puts it all back.

What you type is kept a moment after you pause, and the indicator above the text says whether it was. If you close the page, your text is there when you come back.

Text pasted from elsewhere is checked. A construct the editor does not accept is refused with its line named; in the source view a conversion is offered, shown before it is applied. The list of what the editor accepts is on its own page.

*My edits*, a checkbox in the editor's row, shows the whole document with your changes marked in place; it is enabled after your first edit.

When you are done, choose *Finish editing*: the summary and its buttons stand above your changes. The summary is plain text of several lines — its first line is what every list of versions shows, at most 500 characters; the rest is kept with the version and read whole on its page and in the review page's panel, at most 5,000 characters in all. No line may begin with a word and a colon. Until your first edit, *Release the document* lets the document go and keeps nothing; after it, *Discard my edits…* throws your draft away, once you confirm in its window.

## Images

Choose *Image* in the toolbar to add a picture from your computer, or paste or drop one into the text. PNG, JPEG and GIF are accepted, up to 10 MB, or less where the platform's upload maximum is lower, and at most 40 megapixels and 10,000 pixels a side; a picture refused says why. Each image stands as a paragraph of its own, with a short description that a reader who cannot see it reads instead. Before an image is kept, everything in its file but the picture is taken out — the camera, the place, any comment — except, in a JPEG, which way up it stands. An image that is only a web address is not pasted: copy the image itself and paste it.

## Roles, actions and terms

Select words and choose *Mark* to mark them as a role, an action or a term of this document: pick one the document already declares, or declare a new one by its label. Words already marked take the new one. A role, action or term typed in the source view with a key the document does not declare is declared with your version, labelled with its words. When you finish, and in *My edits*, the roles, actions and terms your draft adds, relabels or removes are listed.

## Mentions

Select words and choose *Mention*, then type another document's number, to mention that document. A reader who may read it sees your words as a link to it; any other reader sees your words alone. Nothing about the document is looked up, so check the number.

## The title page

While you hold the document, the panel on the right of the editor has *Title page*: tick it to give the document a title page, or untick it to take the page away, and write the document's own *Title page text* — plain text, at most 500 characters and 8 lines, its line breaks kept. *Apply* puts both in your draft. They are changes of your draft like your text: the finishing screen, *My edits* and the reviewer's review page list them, and they become part of the document with your version. A version that changes nothing else can be sent too. Undoing an author's edits keeps them, as it keeps a title. Which details the title page shows, and in which order, is the organisation's choice: *Template and styles* tells how it is set.

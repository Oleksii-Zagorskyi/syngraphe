# Reading a document

A document's page shows its text as it reads, never as it is written. It shows the newest version you may see: the **pending edits** if you may read them, otherwise the **accepted edits**, otherwise the **canonical version**. Your own draft is never here; while you hold the document it is a point of its own on the path, shown to you alone. The heading names the version shown: *1.0*, *1.0-1*.

**The path**, above the text, draws the document's versions as points — the canonical version, the latest accepted version, the latest version and, while you hold the document, your draft — joined by arcs of the edits between them. A point opens its version to read. The arc from the canonical version to the latest accepted opens their comparison; the arc of the edits waiting for review opens the review page. Each act stands beside the version it changes: *Edit* on the latest version, *Review the run* on the edits waiting for review, *Freeze* and *Make canonical as N.0…* on the latest accepted version. You see only the points and arcs your rights reach; a reader of the canonical version alone sees no path. **History**, **Info** and **Export** are icons beside the heading.

- **Clause numbers** stand in the margin. `1.1-3` reads as section 1, subsection 1, clause 3: the headings above the clause, a hyphen, then the clause's place in its section. Every paragraph is a clause, including those inside lists, quotes and callouts. A table does not count, and nor does a paragraph that holds only an image. A heading carries its own number, `1.1`, at the start of the heading itself. A paragraph before the first heading carries its place alone. The numbers are worked out every time the text is shown, so they follow the text: an edit that adds a clause renumbers the clauses after it.
- **The outline**, on the left, stands on every page that shows a version and lists the headings of the version it shows, each with its number. A heading jumps to its place in the text; the triangle beside a heading shows or hides the headings under it. Drag the pane's edge to change its width; the width is kept for you, as is the right pane's.
- **Parts**, on the right, lists the document's appendices and attachments in the version you are reading. An appendix opens on a page of its own; an attachment downloads as a file and is never opened by the platform. *Appendices and attachments* tells more.
- **Info**, from its icon, names the canonical version, if the document has one, and the document's state as the version you read shows it.

Each pane closes to a narrow strip with the button at its top, and opens again the same way.

## Mentions and generated views

A **mention** of another document reads as a link to it where you may read that document, and as its words alone where you may not.

**Generated views**, the second button in the right pane, show the **role directory** — each role the document declares or marks, with every heading and clause marking it, linked to its place — and **see also**, each document this one mentions: its number and title where you may read it, the mention's words where not. A role marked without a key declares nothing: it is listed by its words, *not declared* beside them. The system works them out from the version shown.

## Comparing two documents

A document can be compared with another one: *Compare documents…*, on the document list, opens the comparison, which shows the second document's whole text marked against the first's. The document's page does not offer it. *Finding and organising documents* tells how.

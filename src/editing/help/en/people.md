# People and access

**Inviting a person.** An organisation's administrator opens Administration › *Users* and chooses *Invite by email*: an address, and the groups the person joins. The person becomes a member at once and gets an email with a link that sets their password and signs them in; the link works once, for seven days. The page answers the same whether or not the address already has an account somewhere, and the list of users shows each member by address with the date they joined — never whether they have accepted. Inviting a member again sends a fresh link and ends the earlier one. Accepting an invitation sets the password of the account at that address, wherever else it is used.

**A forgotten password.** *Forgot your password?* on the sign-in page asks for an address and always answers the same: if the address belongs to an account with a password, an email with a link is sent to it. The link sets a new password, signs you in and signs you out everywhere else; it works once, for an hour. A link used ends every other link you were sent. Asking again too soon from the same place is not answered with another email.

**Blocking a person.** On Administration › *Users*, *Block…* closes the organisation to a member: they reach nothing in it, and every document they hold or have reserved there is released, their draft kept and the document free for others. An administrator of several organisations chooses which of them to block the person in. The organisation's last active administrator cannot be blocked while someone else remains a member. *Lift the block* lets the person back in, with the groups they had. An invitation the block ended stays ended: invite the person again for a fresh link.

**Blocking your own account.** *Block my account…*, beside your address at the top of every page, closes every organisation you belong to and releases every document you hold, your drafts kept. You can still sign in, and reach nothing. Nothing in the platform lifts this block: a platform administrator asks the installation's operator, who lifts it in the database. The platform's last administrator cannot block their own account.

**Several organisations.** A person who belongs to more than one organisation chooses where to work in the switcher beside the organisation's name. On an organisation's own address, choosing another takes you to that organisation's address, where you sign in; an organisation without an address of its own opens on the platform's address, which asks you to confirm the switch.

**For platform administrators.** Administration › *Organisations* blocks an organisation — its members reach nothing in it, every document held in it is released — and lifts the block — the invitations the block ended stay ended — and appoints an administrator among its active members. Administration › *Platform administrators* grants the role to a registered person and revokes it; the last platform administrator keeps it. Platform administrators are told when a person blocked in one organisation is still active in another, and when an organisation is left without an active administrator.

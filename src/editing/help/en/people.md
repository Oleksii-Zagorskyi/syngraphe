# People and access

**Inviting a person.** An organisation's administrator opens Administration › *Users* and chooses *Invite by email*: an address, and the groups the person joins. The person becomes a member at once and gets an email with a link that sets their password and signs them in; the link works once, for seven days. The page answers the same whether or not the address already has an account somewhere, and the list of users shows each member by address with the date they joined — never whether they have accepted. Inviting a member again sends a fresh link and ends the earlier one. Accepting an invitation sets the password of the account at that address, wherever else it is used.

**A forgotten password.** *Forgot your password?* on the sign-in page asks for an address and always answers the same: if the address belongs to an account with a password, an email with a link is sent to it. The link sets a new password, signs you in and signs you out everywhere else; it works once, for an hour. A link used ends every other link you were sent. Asking again too soon from the same place is not answered with another email.

**Blocking a person.** On Administration › *Users*, *Block…* closes the organisation to a member: they reach nothing in it, and every document they hold or have reserved there is released, their draft kept and the document free for others. An administrator of several organisations chooses which of them to block the person in. The organisation's last active administrator cannot be blocked while someone else remains a member. *Lift the block* lets the person back in, with the groups they had. An invitation the block ended stays ended: invite the person again for a fresh link.

**A member's page.** On Administration › *Users*, a member's address opens their page: their state in the organisation, the groups they belong to — *Add* and *Remove* change them there, as *members…* does — and, per folder, each document their groups and its links give them rights on, with those rights ticked. An administrator's page says in one line that they read every document. A blocked member may be taken out of a group but added to none, and their page says none of it applies now. A person's own block of their account never shows here.

**Your account.** The person icon at the end of the menu opens your account page; pointing at it shows your name — your address until you give one. It shows your address; your **display name**, with *Apply*; and the interface language — Ukrainian or English — with *Apply*. *Sign out* stands there too. Your choice of language is your own: every page you open and every email the platform sends you follow it; until you choose, you see the installation's default. An invitation always comes in the default. A document's language is its own, whatever yours is.

**Your display name** is how the organisation's pages name you — as the author of an edit, the holder of a document, in histories and notifications; administration pages name you by your address. You choose it yourself, and it is unique in each organisation you belong to: a name another member already uses — whatever its capital letters or its spaces — is refused, and the page does not say where. Joining an organisation, by an invitation or otherwise, waits while your name is already used there: change it here first. Until you give one, pages name you *anonymous editor*; once given, it can be changed but not removed.

**Blocking your own account.** *Block my account…*, at the foot of your account page, closes every organisation you belong to and releases every document you hold, your drafts kept. You can still sign in, and reach nothing. Nothing in the platform lifts this block: a platform administrator asks the installation's operator, who lifts it in the database. The platform's last administrator cannot block their own account.

**Several organisations.** A person who belongs to more than one organisation sees its name in the menu as a link: it opens the list of their organisations, and choosing one switches to it. With one organisation the name is plain. On an organisation's own address, choosing another takes you to that organisation's address, where you sign in; an organisation without an address of its own opens on the platform's address, which asks you to confirm the switch.

**For platform administrators.** Administration › *Organisations* blocks an organisation — its members reach nothing in it, every document held in it is released — and lifts the block — the invitations the block ended stay ended — and appoints an administrator among its active members. Administration › *Platform administrators* grants the role to a registered person and revokes it; the last platform administrator keeps it. Platform administrators are told when a person blocked in one organisation is still active in another, and when an organisation is left without an active administrator.

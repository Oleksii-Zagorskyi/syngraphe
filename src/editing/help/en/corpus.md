# Finding and organising documents

## Folders and the list

The document list shows one folder at a time. The tree on its left holds the folders, starting with the organisation itself; the folder you are in is in bold. It shows only the folders that hold, at any depth, a document you may read; administrators see every folder. The triangle beside a folder shows or hides the folders under it. Drag the edge between the tree and the list to change its width; the width is kept for you.

The list can be sorted by number or by title, either way round: choose a column's heading. Each row shows the document's type, its canonical version, the latest version you may see beyond it, whether it has appendices and attachments, ✎ to open it for editing where you may edit it, and ⧉ to copy it. *Being edited* and *In review* group their documents by folder in the same way.

A folder only says where a document appears. Moving a document between folders changes nothing in the document itself or in who may read it.

## Search and the type filter

The search box finds documents by the title of the document or of one of its appendices, in any case, never by the text. It finds only documents you may read, and an appendix only as the version you read names it. The results are grouped by folder, with the tree beside them cut to those folders.

Beside the box, the type filter lists the types of the documents you may open. Choosing one finds every document of that type, in every folder. The filter is not shown when those documents carry fewer than two types.

## Copying a document

⧉ *Copy*, on a document's row of the list, makes a new document from a version of it you may see — the pending edits, the accepted edits or the canonical version — under the title you give, in a folder you choose. The copy keeps the source's language and title page, its images, and the roles, actions and terms the copied text marks. Choose which appendices go with it: none, all or some; they are numbered from 1 in the copy. Attachments are not copied, and nor is the history: the copy starts from one version. Only you and the administrators can reach it until an administrator links groups to it. You need *create and edit* to copy; an archived document is not copied.

If the version changes while the copy's page is open, the copy is refused and the page shows the version as it now stands. If a copy stops before it is finished, nothing of it can be seen: copy again. Administrators are told of each copy, and the copy's *Info* page shows them its source: the document, the version and when it was copied, and by whom.

## Comparing two documents

*Compare documents…*, on the document list, asks the two documents' numbers and opens the comparison; the document's page does not offer it. The page shows the second document's whole text with its changes against the first's marked in place. *From* and *to* choose a version of each; without a *to*, the second document's newest version you may see is compared. Each document's attachments are listed apart, under its number and version. You need to be able to read both documents, and each version is one its own document lets you see.

## For administrators

**Document types.** *Administration › Document types* adds a type, renames it and retires it. A type's name is unique in the organisation, whatever its case. A retired type stays on the documents that have it but is no longer offered for a new document or a copy. A type no document carries can be deleted, except the type the organisation started with. The last type not retired can be neither retired nor deleted. A document's type is changed on its *Info* page.

**Folders.** *Administration › Folders* creates a folder, renames it, deletes it when it is empty, and moves documents between folders. A folder's name is unique among the folders beside it, whatever its case. The top folder is the organisation's and keeps its name.

**Archive and restore.** *Archive*, under *More* on a document's page, puts it away: only administrators can still open it, read only, with an *archived* tag; every act on it is refused until it is restored. Its group links stay and apply again on *Restore*. Whoever was editing it is told, and their draft is kept, to be taken back after a restore. *Administration › Archive* lists the archived documents, newest first, each with *Restore*.

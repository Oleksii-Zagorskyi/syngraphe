# Records Retention and Destruction Policy

## Purpose and scope

This policy sets how long the Organisation keeps its records and how it destroys them.
It applies to every record of the Organisation, on paper, on microfilm or in electronic form, and to every employee who holds one.

## Definition of a record

A record is a document or data set, on paper, on microfilm or in electronic form, that the Organisation created or received in its work and that evidences a decision, a transaction or an obligation.
Drafts, personal notes and duplicate copies of a record in the same form are not records, and this policy does not govern them.

## Types of record and their owners

Every record belongs to exactly one of the five types below.
A record that fits the description of more than one type belongs to the type that comes first in this section.

- Governance records are board minutes, board resolutions, approved policies and the Destruction Log, and the Secretary owns them.
- Financial records are invoices, payment orders, ledgers and financial statements, and the Finance Lead owns them.
- Personnel records are records about a person's employment with the Organisation, and the People Lead owns them.
- Contract records are signed agreements with outside parties and their amendments, and the Legal Counsel owns them.
- Operational records are all records that belong to none of the four types above, and the head of the unit that created or received the record owns it.

The Records Manager owns the operational records of a unit that no longer exists.

## Retention periods

- Governance records are kept permanently.
- Financial records are kept for 7 years from the end of the financial year in which they were created or received.
- Personnel records are kept for 10 years from the date on which the person's employment ends.
- Contract records are kept for 6 years from the date on which the agreement ends.
- Operational records are kept for 3 years from the date on which they were created or received.

The retention periods are checked against the law once every three years.

## Legal hold

A legal hold is a written instruction that suspends the destruction of named records.
The Legal Counsel places a legal hold within 2 calendar days of learning of a dispute, an audit or an investigation to which the records may relate.
The Legal Counsel lifts the legal hold within 5 calendar days after the matter is closed.
The Legal Counsel notifies the owner of the records and the Records Manager in writing on the day a legal hold is placed and on the day it is lifted.
No one destroys a record under a legal hold, even if its retention period has ended.

## Destruction

The owner proposes a record for destruction to the Records Manager in writing within 30 calendar days after its retention period ends.
If the record is under a legal hold when its retention period ends, or is placed under one after that day and before it is destroyed, the owner instead proposes it within 30 calendar days after the legal hold is lifted.
The Records Manager answers the proposal in writing within 10 calendar days.
The Records Manager refuses the proposal if the retention period has not ended or if the record is under a legal hold, and approves it otherwise.
After a refusal the owner proposes the record again within the time limits of this section.
For records that the Records Manager owns, the Legal Counsel answers the proposal instead of the Records Manager, under the same rules.
An approval lapses on the day a legal hold is placed on the record.

Within 15 calendar days after approval, the owner shreds a paper record and erases an electronic record so that it cannot be restored.
A record held in both forms is one record and is destroyed in both forms.
When the owner destroys a record, the owner also erases or shreds every copy of it.
A record whose proposal the Records Manager has refused is under a legal hold until the owner proposes it again.

## Destruction Log

The owner notifies the Records Manager in writing within 5 calendar days after a record is destroyed.
The Records Manager enters the destruction in the Destruction Log within 5 calendar days after the notification.
Each entry states the type of the record, its description, its owner, the date of approval, the date of destruction and the method used.

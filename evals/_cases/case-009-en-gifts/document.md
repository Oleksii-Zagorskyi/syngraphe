# Code of Conduct, Section 7: Gifts and Hospitality

## 7.1 Scope

Section 7 applies to every employee of the Organisation, including the Chief Executive.
It covers every gift and all hospitality that a third party offers to an employee, or that an employee offers to a third party, in connection with the Organisation's work.
For a gift or hospitality that the Compliance Officer is offered, receives or gives, the Chief Executive acts in place of the Compliance Officer.

## 7.2 Definitions

A third party is any person or body outside the Organisation.
Hospitality is a meal, a drink, entertainment, travel or accommodation that the recipient does not pay for.
A gift is any other thing of value that the recipient does not pay for, including goods, vouchers, discounts and services.
A money gift is a gift of money or of a voucher that can be exchanged for money.
The value of a gift or of hospitality is its retail price for one recipient on one occasion.
A public official is a person who works for a state body, a municipal body or a court.

## 7.3 Value limits

The gift limit is 50 euros.
The hospitality limit is 120 euros.
An employee may, without approval, accept or give a gift at or below the gift limit and hospitality at or below the hospitality limit.
An employee must not accept or give a gift or hospitality above its limit unless an exception has been approved under Section 7.5.
An employee must not accept or give a money gift of any value, and no exception may be approved for it.
When an employee cannot establish the value, the employee asks the Compliance Officer, who sets the value in writing within two working days.

## 7.4 Declaration

The Compliance Officer keeps the Gifts and Hospitality Register.
A gift in the form of a meal, travel or accommodation is declared with the name of the third party that paid for it.
An employee declares in the Gifts and Hospitality Register every gift and all hospitality that the employee accepts or gives, whatever its value.
The employee makes the declaration within five working days of the acceptance or the giving.
An employee need not declare an offer that the employee refused.

## 7.5 Exceptions

An employee who wishes to accept or give a gift or hospitality above its limit sends a written request to the Compliance Officer beforehand.
The Compliance Officer approves or refuses the request in writing within three working days.
The Compliance Officer approves a request only when the employee shows a business reason and the Organisation has no pending purchase or contract decision that concerns the third party.
The role that decides the request records the decision in the Gifts and Hospitality Register on the same day.
Until a request is approved, the employee treats it as refused.

## 7.6 A gift or hospitality above the limit

When an employee has received, without an approved exception, a gift above the gift limit or a money gift, the employee hands it, unused, to the Compliance Officer and declares it, both within two working days of learning that it is such a gift.
The Compliance Officer returns the gift to the third party within ten working days.
When return is impossible, the Compliance Officer donates the gift to a registered charity and records the donation in the Gifts and Hospitality Register.
When an employee learns after the occasion that a gift given or hospitality received or given was above its limit, or that a gift given was a money gift, the employee records it in the declaration within ten working days of learning it.
When the hospitality was received, the employee pays the third party the excess within the same period.

## 7.7 Public officials

An employee must not give a gift or hospitality to a public official.
The only exception is refreshments worth 15 euros or less for each public official, served at a working meeting on the Organisation's premises.
Refreshments for a public official are limited to 10 euros for each official.
Section 7.7 applies to giving to public officials instead of Section 7.3, and no exception under Section 7.5 may be approved for it.
The employee who hosts the meeting declares the refreshments under Section 7.4.
A gift or hospitality that a public official offers to an employee is governed by Sections 7.3 to 7.6.

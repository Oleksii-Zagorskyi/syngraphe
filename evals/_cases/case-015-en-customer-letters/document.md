# Guideline on Writing Letters to Customers

## Purpose and scope

This guideline helps the Customer Service Department of the Organisation write letters that a customer understands at once.
A customer is a person or a company that holds a signed service agreement with the Organisation.
A letter is any written text sent to a customer on paper or by email.
A message is any written text that a customer sends to the Organisation.
A complaint is a message in which the customer says that the Organisation did something wrong.
The guideline applies to every letter written by an Adviser or a Team Lead of the Department.
An Adviser or a Team Lead who writes a letter is its author.
Letters to anyone who is not a customer are outside this guideline.

## Tone and plain language

The author writes directly, calmly and without blame.
The author uses short sentences and everyday words.
The author writes "we" for the Organisation and "you" for the customer.

## Structure of a letter

The author builds every letter from the following parts, in this order.

- A subject line that names the matter in ten words or fewer.
- A greeting, written as the section "Addressing the customer" describes.
- An opening sentence that gives the main answer or the main news.
- The reasons and details behind that opening sentence.
- The next step, with the role that takes it and the date.
- A closing line with the telephone number and email address of the Department.
- A signature, written as the section "Signatures" describes.

## Addressing the customer

The author takes the name and title from the customer record.
For a person whose record holds a title, the author writes "Dear", the title and the family name.
For a person whose record holds no title, the author writes "Dear", the first name and the family name.
For a company, the author addresses the contact person named in the customer record, following the two rules above.
For a company whose record names no contact person, the author writes "Dear Customer".
A customer who has asked for an offer and has not yet signed a service agreement is addressed in the same way.

## Check by a second person

A letter is checked before sending when it does at least one of three things: it refuses something the customer asked for, it states a sum of money, or it answers a complaint.
Every other letter is sent without a check.
An interim letter, described in the section "Response times", is also sent without a check, whatever its subject.
When the author is an Adviser, the Team Lead of that Adviser's team appoints another Adviser of the same team as the checker.
When the author is a Team Lead, the Head of the Department appoints the Team Lead of another team as the checker.
The checker confirms the letter or returns it with corrections within one working day.
When the author and the checker do not agree, the Head of the Department decides the wording.
The author sends the letter only after the checker confirms it or the Head of the Department decides its wording.

## Signatures

The author signs every letter and no other role signs it.
The signature gives the author's full name, the author's role and the name of the Department.

## Response times

The Head of the Department assigns each message to an author on the working day the Organisation receives it.
Time limits are counted in working days and start on the working day after the Organisation receives the message.
The author answers a complaint within ten working days.
The author answers any other message within five working days.
The time taken by the check is part of these limits.
When the author cannot give a full answer within the limit, the author sends an interim letter within the same limit.
The interim letter states the date of the full answer, which is no later than twenty working days after the start of the limit.
The Team Lead reviews all open messages of the team every Monday and reassigns any message whose limit is at risk.
A letter that does not answer a message has no time limit under this guideline.

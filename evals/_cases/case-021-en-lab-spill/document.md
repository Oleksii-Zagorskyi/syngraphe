# Standard Operating Procedure: Cleaning a Chemical Spill in a Laboratory

## 1. Scope

This procedure applies to every spill of a liquid or solid chemical substance in a laboratory room of the Organisation.
It does not apply to releases of gas or to spills of biological or radioactive material.

## 2. Classes of spill

The Safety Manager keeps the chemical register, which marks each substance as low-hazard or high-hazard.
The discoverer is the person who first sees the spill.
A small spill is a spill that meets all of the following conditions.

- It consists of one substance that the chemical register marks as low-hazard.
- Its quantity is 500 millilitres or less for a liquid, or 500 grams or less for a solid.
- No person is injured or contaminated by the substance.

A large spill is every spill that is not a small spill.
A spill of one litre or less of one low-hazard liquid, with no person injured or contaminated by it, is a small spill.
The discoverer classes the spill at once.

## 3. Personal protective equipment

A trained worker is a laboratory worker who holds the spill training certificate of the Organisation.
A trained worker who cleans a small spill wears a laboratory coat, safety goggles, nitrile gloves and closed shoes.
A member of the Spill Response Team who cleans a large spill wears a chemical-resistant suit, chemical-resistant boots and a full-face respirator.
A stocked spill kit is kept in each laboratory room.

## 4. Who cleans and who is called

Only a trained worker may clean a small spill, and only the Spill Response Team may clean a large spill.
A discoverer of a small spill who is a trained worker cleans it.
A trained worker who is alone in the laboratory calls the emergency line 4400 before cleaning a small spill.
Every other discoverer calls the emergency line 4400, which the Duty Safety Officer answers at all hours.
The Duty Safety Officer sends a trained worker to a small spill.

## 5. Steps for a small spill

The steps follow in this order.

- The discoverer warns every person in the room to keep away from the spill.
- The trained worker puts on the equipment named in section 3.
- The trained worker covers a liquid with the absorbent, or collects a solid with the scoop, from the spill kit.
- The trained worker places the used absorbent, the collected substance and the used gloves in a waste bag from the spill kit.
- The trained worker disposes of the waste bag as section 7 requires.

A trained worker who finds at any step that the spill is a large spill stops and follows section 6 as the discoverer.

## 6. Steps for a large spill

The steps follow in this order.

- The discoverer warns every person in the room, and every person leaves the room.
- The discoverer closes the door and calls the emergency line 4400.
- The Duty Safety Officer sends the Spill Response Team, and also calls the medical post of the Organisation if a person is injured or contaminated.
- The discoverer keeps every person out of the room until the Spill Response Team arrives.
- The Spill Response Team puts on the equipment named in section 3 and cleans the spill.
- The Team Leader, who leads the Spill Response Team, tells the Duty Safety Officer that cleaning is complete.
- The Duty Safety Officer inspects the room and reopens it.

## 7. Waste disposal

All material collected from a spill is hazardous waste.
The trained worker, for a small spill, or the Team Leader, for a large spill, seals the waste bag and labels it with the substance and the date.
The same person delivers the waste bag to the hazardous waste store on the day of the spill, and the Waste Store Keeper records it in the waste log.

## 8. Reporting the spill

The trained worker who cleaned a small spill submits a spill report to the Laboratory Manager within 24 hours after the cleaning ends.
The Laboratory Manager forwards that report to the Safety Manager within five working days.
The Laboratory Manager keeps a stocked spill kit in every laboratory room and restocks it within one working day after each small spill.
The Team Leader submits a spill report on a large spill to the Safety Manager within 24 hours after the room is reopened.
The Safety Manager records every spill report in the spill register.

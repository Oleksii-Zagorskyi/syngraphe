# Spending Approval Matrix

## 1. Purpose and scope

This matrix states who may approve spending of the Organisation's money.
It applies to every spending request raised by an employee of the Organisation, whatever the amount.
The finance director and the chief executive are employees.
Every other employee belongs to a department.
The board is not an employee and raises no spending requests.
Payment of salaries and taxes is outside this matrix.

## 2. Terms

A purchase is the goods or services that the Organisation acquires from one supplier for one purpose.
A spending request is a written request to commit the Organisation's money to one purchase.
The requester is the employee who raises the spending request.
The amount is the total that the Organisation will pay for the purchase, taxes included, over the full term of the commitment.
Where the commitment has no end date, the amount is the total payable in its first twelve months, and the requester raises a new spending request for each following twelve months.
The approver is the role that this matrix names to decide a spending request.

## 3. Amount bands

The approver depends on the amount, as follows.

- From 0 to 2,000 euros inclusive, the approver is the team lead of the requester's team.
- Above 2,500 and up to 15,000 euros inclusive, the approver is the head of the requester's department.
- Above 15,000 and up to 75,000 euros inclusive, the approver is the finance director.
- Above 75,000 and up to 300,000 euros inclusive, the approver is the chief executive.
- Above 300,000 euros, the approver is the board.

The order of roles, from lowest to highest, is team lead, head of department, finance director, chief executive, the board.
Where the requester belongs to no team, the head of the requester's department is the approver for the first band.

## 4. Requests by a team lead or a head of department

No requester approves their own spending request.
Where the requester is a team lead or a head of department and the amount falls in a band above the requester's own band, section 3 applies unchanged.
Where the amount falls in the requester's own band or in a lower band, the approver is instead the role next above the requester's role in the order in section 3.

## 5. Requests by the finance director and the chief executive

Section 5 applies to these two requesters instead of sections 3 and 4.
The chief executive approves a spending request by the finance director of up to 300,000 euros inclusive.
The board approves a spending request by the finance director above 300,000 euros.
The board approves every spending request by the chief executive, whatever the amount.

## 6. Duties of the requester and the approver

The requester obtains approval before committing the Organisation to the purchase.
The approver approves or refuses the spending request in writing and states the reason for a refusal.
Where the amount rises after approval, the requester raises a new spending request for the full new amount, and it replaces the earlier one.
The finance director keeps a register of all decided spending requests.

## 7. No splitting of a purchase

No employee raises a spending request for only part of a purchase.
A spending request raised within 30 calendar days after another spending request for the same supplier and the same purpose belongs to the same purchase as that other request, whoever raises them.
An approver who finds that a spending request covers only part of a purchase refuses it and tells the requester to raise one spending request for the full amount of the purchase.
That spending request replaces every earlier spending request for the same purchase.

## 8. Absence of an approver

An approver is absent when unable to act for two or more consecutive working days.
A spending request waits for an approver who is unable to act for a shorter time.
Where the approver is absent, the role next above the approver's role in the order in section 3 decides instead.
When the chief executive is absent, the finance director decides each spending request that the chief executive approves, instead of the board; when both are absent, the board decides.
No substitute acts for the board, and a spending request for the board waits for its next meeting.

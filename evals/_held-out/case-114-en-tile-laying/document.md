# Installation Instruction: Laying Ceramic Tiles on Interior Walls and Floors

## Purpose and scope

This instruction sets out how the Organisation lays ceramic tiles on interior walls and interior floors with cement-based adhesive.
The base is the wall or floor that receives the tiles.
A large tile is a tile whose longest side is more than 300 mm, and a small tile is a tile whose longest side is 300 mm or less.

## Roles

The Tiler carries out the work and answers to the Site Foreman.
The Site Foreman and the Quality Inspector answer to the Works Manager.

## Materials and tools

Before work starts, the Site Foreman issues to the Tiler tiles from a single production batch for each wall or floor, adhesive in 25 kg bags, primer, levelling compound, grout and spacers of 2 mm and 3 mm.
The Tiler uses a trowel with 8 mm notches for small tiles and a trowel with 10 mm notches for large tiles.
Mixed adhesive stays usable for 3 hours after the second mixing.

## Surface preparation

The Tiler removes dust, grease, loose plaster and old coatings from the base.
The Tiler then measures the flatness of the base with a 2 m straightedge.
If the deviation is 3 mm or less, the Tiler primes the base.
If the deviation is more than 3 mm, the Tiler applies levelling compound, waits 24 hours and measures again, repeating this until the deviation is 3 mm or less.

The Tiler applies one coat of primer to the whole base and waits 4 hours.
The Tiler mixes adhesive only after those 4 hours have ended.

## Adhesive mixing and open time

The Tiler adds one 25 kg bag of adhesive to 6 litres of water, mixes for 3 minutes, waits 5 minutes and mixes again for 1 minute.
The Tiler discards any adhesive that remains 2 hours after the second mixing ended.

The Tiler spreads adhesive on the base with the notched trowel and places each tile within 20 minutes of spreading the adhesive under it.
If 20 minutes have passed, or if a skin has formed on the adhesive, the Tiler scrapes the adhesive off the base and spreads fresh adhesive.

## Laying order

Where a room has both walls and a floor to tile, the Tiler completes the walls first.

On a wall, the Tiler marks a vertical centre line and fixes a level batten whose top edge is at the height of the top of the bottom row.
The Tiler lays the rows upward from the batten, working in each row from the centre line toward the corners.
The Tiler removes the batten 24 hours after laying the row directly above it and then lays the bottom row.

On a floor, the Tiler marks two centre lines that cross at a right angle.
The Tiler starts at the crossing and completes the half farthest from the door before the half nearest the door.

For large tiles, the Tiler works from the corner farthest from the door instead.

## Joint width

The Tiler sets the joints with spacers on every side of every tile.
The joint width is 2 mm for small tiles and 3 mm for large tiles, a large tile here being one of 600 mm or more.
The Tiler checks each completed row with the spirit level before starting the next row.

## Grouting

The waiting time before grouting is 24 hours for a wall and 48 hours for a floor, counted from the laying of the last tile of that wall or floor.

When the waiting time has ended, the Site Foreman checks that the joints are clean and that the height difference between neighbouring tiles is 1 mm or less.
If both conditions are met, the Site Foreman records the release for grouting.
If either condition is not met, the Tiler lays the affected tiles again under the section "Adhesive mixing and open time", and the waiting time for that wall or floor starts anew.

After the release, the Tiler removes the spacers and presses the grout into the joints with a rubber float.

## Checks

The Quality Inspector carries out the final check 24 hours or more after grouting and confirms each of the following.

- The deviation of the finished tiling under a 2 m straightedge is 2 mm or less.
- The height difference between neighbouring tiles is 1 mm or less.
- The joint width differs from the width set in the section "Joint width" by 0.5 mm or less.
- No tile sounds hollow when tapped.

If every point is confirmed, the Quality Inspector signs the acceptance record.
If any point is not confirmed, the Quality Inspector lists the defects, the Tiler lays the affected tiles again under the section "Adhesive mixing and open time", and that wall or floor passes again through the section "Grouting", from the start of the waiting time, and then through the final check.

# Standard on Meeting Minutes

## Purpose and scope

This standard sets how the Organisation records its meetings in minutes.
It applies to every meeting that employees of the Organisation hold in the course of their work, whether in person or by video link.
The Head of the Administrative Office supervises compliance with this standard and reports on it to the Director General once every quarter.

## Terms

A participant is any person who attends a meeting, in person or by video link, for all or part of it.
The chair is the participant who presides over the meeting.
The secretary is the participant who writes the minutes of the meeting.
A working day is any day from Monday to Friday that is not a public holiday.

## Meetings that require minutes

The secretary writes minutes for each of the following meetings.

- Every meeting of the Management Board.
- Every meeting of a standing committee of the Organisation.
- Any other meeting at which the participants make a decision that commits the Organisation's money, changes an approved document of the Organisation, or assigns a task with a deadline.

A meeting that falls under none of these three lines requires no minutes, and this standard places no further duty on its participants.

## The secretary

The Board Secretary is the secretary of every meeting of the Management Board and of every meeting of a standing committee.
When the Board Secretary is absent from such a meeting, the chair appoints another participant as secretary before the meeting opens.
At any other meeting that requires minutes, the chair appoints a participant as secretary no later than the moment the first decision that makes minutes necessary is made.
The chair never appoints himself or herself as secretary.

## Content of minutes

The secretary records the following in the minutes.

- The date of the meeting, the time it opened and closed, and the place or the video link used.
- The name of the body that met, or the subject of the meeting where no standing body met.
- The name and position of each participant, with a note of who was the chair and who was the secretary, and the names of invited guests listed apart.
- The agenda, or the list of matters discussed where no agenda was set in advance.
- A short summary of the discussion on each matter.
- The exact wording of each decision.
- The number of votes for, against and abstaining on each decision that was put to a vote.
- Each task assigned, with the person responsible for it and its deadline.
- The dissenting opinion of any participant who asks the secretary to record it.

## Circulation of the draft

The secretary sends the draft minutes of a meeting held in person to all participants by the Organisation's e-mail within three working days after the day of the meeting.
Each participant may send comments on the draft to the secretary within two working days after the day the draft was sent.
A participant who sends no comments within that period is treated as having none.
The secretary amends the draft to take in each comment that the secretary accepts.
The secretary passes each comment that the secretary does not accept to the chair, and the chair decides the final wording of the passage concerned.
A participant whose comment the chair rejects may hand the secretary a written remark, and the secretary attaches that remark to the minutes.

## Approval of minutes

The secretary prepares the final text of the minutes and signs it within two working days after the comment period described in the section "Circulation of the draft" ends.
The secretary sends the signed final text to the chair on the day of signing.
The chair approves the minutes by signing them within two working days after the day the chair receives the final text.
When the chair is absent for those two working days, the secretary approves the minutes instead.
The minutes are approved from the moment they are signed in approval.
Within one working day after the day of approval, the secretary sends the approved minutes to all participants and to every person who is assigned a task in the minutes and was not a participant.

## Storage

Within one working day after the day of approval, the secretary also hands the approved minutes, with any attached written remarks, to the Records Clerk of the Administrative Office.
The Records Clerk enters the minutes in the register of minutes, gives them a serial number and places them in the Organisation's electronic archive on the day of receipt.
The Records Clerk keeps minutes of the Management Board and of standing committees for ten years from the date of the meeting.
The Records Clerk keeps the minutes of every other meeting for five years from the date of the meeting.
The Records Clerk opens access to stored minutes for the participants of the meeting, for every person assigned a task in them, for the Head of the Administrative Office and for the Director General.
The Records Clerk opens access for any other employee only on the written permission of the Director General.
When the storage period of minutes ends, the Records Clerk deletes them from the archive after receiving the written consent of the Head of the Administrative Office.

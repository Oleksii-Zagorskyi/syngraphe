# Rules for Handling Personal Data Requests

## 1. Purpose and scope

These rules set out how the Organisation handles a request from a person about the personal data the Organisation holds about that person.
The person who makes a request is called the requester.
These rules cover three kinds of request.

- An access request asks for a copy of the personal data held about the requester.
- A correction request asks the Organisation to change data that the requester states is inaccurate or incomplete.
- An erasure request asks the Organisation to delete the personal data held about the requester.

Where one letter asks for more than one kind, the Records Clerk registers each kind as a separate request.
In these rules a day is a calendar day.

## 2. Roles

The Records Clerk registers each request, checks the identity of the requester and sends all notices and replies.
The Data Protection Officer decides each request, checks the log every Monday for requests close to their time limit, and answers to the Managing Director for that work.
The Head of Legal approves each extension and reviews each refusal.
Where the requester is the Records Clerk, the Data Protection Officer or the Head of Legal, the Managing Director appoints another employee to perform the duties of that role for that request.

## 3. Submitting and registering a request

The requester submits a request in writing, either to the data protection mailbox or on paper at the reception desk.
An employee who receives a written request by any other route forwards it to the Records Clerk within 1 day.
An employee who is asked orally directs the person to the two written routes.
The Records Clerk registers each written request in the log described in section 8 within 2 days of the day the Records Clerk receives it.

## 4. Identity check

Within 2 days of registration the Records Clerk asks the requester for the items that confirm identity.
A requester who is a current employee supplies their staff number from their work mailbox.
Any other requester supplies a copy of an identity document and one contact detail that matches the records of the Organisation.
Where the items arrive and confirm identity within 10 days of the day the Records Clerk asks, the Records Clerk records the confirmation date in the log.
Where identity is not confirmed within those 10 days, the Data Protection Officer refuses the request under section 6.

## 5. Time limit and extension

The Data Protection Officer decides the request, and the Records Clerk sends the reply, within 30 days of the confirmation date.
The Data Protection Officer may propose one extension of up to 30 further days where the data sits in the records of more than three units or must be retrieved from the backup archive.
The Head of Legal approves or rejects the proposal within 3 days of receiving it.
Where the extension is approved, the Records Clerk notifies the requester of the new date and the reason before the first 30 days end.

## 6. Refusal and its reasons

The Data Protection Officer grants or refuses each request, in whole or in part.
The Data Protection Officer refuses a request only for one of the following reasons.

- The identity of the requester was not confirmed within the period set in section 4.
- For an access request, the copy would disclose the personal data of another person and that data cannot be removed from the copy.
- For a correction request, the requester supplied no evidence that the data is inaccurate or incomplete.
- For an erasure request, the Retention Schedule of the Organisation requires the data to be kept.

Where a reason applies to only part of the data, the Data Protection Officer refuses that part and grants the remainder.
The Records Clerk sends each refusal in writing, stating the reason and the right to ask for a review within 14 days under section 7.
The Records Clerk sends a refusal for unconfirmed identity within 5 days after the period set in section 4 ends, instead of within the time limit in section 5.

## 7. Carrying out a decision and review

For a granted access request, the Records Clerk sends the copy in electronic form.
For a granted correction request, the head of each unit that holds the data makes the change and confirms it to the Records Clerk in writing before the reply is sent.
The requester may ask the Records Clerk in writing for a review of a refusal within 20 days of the day the refusal is sent.
The Head of Legal upholds or overturns the refusal within 15 days of the day the Records Clerk receives the review request.
The Records Clerk notifies the requester of the outcome within 2 days of the review decision.
Where the refusal is overturned, the Data Protection Officer grants the request, and the time limit in section 5 starts again on the day of the review decision.

## 8. Log of requests

The Records Clerk keeps one log of requests for the whole Organisation.
For each request the Records Clerk records the registration number, the date of receipt, the kind of request, the confirmation date, any extension, the decision, the reason for any refusal, the reply date and any review outcome.
The log is checked every Monday for requests that are close to their time limit.

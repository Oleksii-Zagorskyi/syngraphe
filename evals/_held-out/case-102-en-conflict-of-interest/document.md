# Code of Conduct, Part 6: Declarations of Interest

## Section 1. Scope

This part applies to every employee of the Organisation.
Members of the Governing Board are not employees and are not covered by this part.
A working day is any day from Monday to Friday.

## Section 2. Interests to be declared

A counterparty is any entity that supplies the Organisation, buys from it, competes with it, or has submitted an offer to it.
A close relative is the employee's spouse or partner, parent, child or sibling, and the spouse or partner of any of these persons.
A business tie is a share of any size in a counterparty, a management post in a counterparty, or paid work for a counterparty.
An interest is any of the following.

- A business tie held by the employee.
- A business tie held by a close relative of the employee.
- The employment of a close relative by the Organisation, or a close relative's application for a post in the Organisation.
- A gift that Section 6 treats as an interest.

## Section 3. The declaration form and when it is filed

An employee declares on Form D-6.
On the form the employee states each interest that exists and each ended interest whose end the employee has not yet declared, the person who holds it, the counterparty or post concerned, and the dates on which the interest began and, if it has ended, ended.
An employee who has no interest to state states this on the form.
The employee signs the form and sends it on the same day to the Ethics Officer and to the employee's line manager.

An employee files three kinds of declaration.

- The employee files an initial declaration within 10 working days of the first day of employment.
- The employee files an annual declaration between 1 and 31 January of every calendar year after the year in which the employment began.
- The employee files an event declaration within 5 working days of the day on which the employee learns that an interest has begun, changed or ended.

A late declaration is recorded as late and followed up with the employee.

## Section 4. Receipt and decision

The Ethics Officer makes no decision on a declaration that states neither an existing interest nor an ended interest, and only records it under Section 5.
On a declaration that states at least one interest, existing or ended, the Ethics Officer decides within 10 working days of receipt.
The Ethics Officer makes one of three decisions for each interest stated.

- The interest creates no conflict, and no measure is needed.
- The interest creates a conflict that is managed by measures, which the decision lists.
- The interest creates a conflict that cannot be managed, and the employee must end the interest within the period that the decision sets.

The Ethics Officer sends the decision in writing to the employee and to the employee's line manager.
For a declaration of a head of department, the head's deputy, who reports to the head, acts as the line manager under Sections 3 and 4.
The line manager puts the measures into effect within the period above and confirms this in writing to the Ethics Officer.
From the day of filing until the decision or, where the decision is that the employee must end the interest, until the interest ends, the line manager assigns every matter that concerns the declared interest to another employee.

The Ethics Officer answers to the Chair of the Governing Board for decisions made under this section.
The Ethics Officer's own declarations go to the Chair of the Governing Board instead of the Ethics Officer and the line manager.
The Chair of the Governing Board then performs every duty that Sections 4 and 5 give to the Ethics Officer and to the line manager.
The Chair of the Governing Board reports each decision on the Ethics Officer's declarations to the Governing Board at its next meeting.

## Section 5. Register of declarations

The Ethics Officer keeps the register of declarations.
Within 3 working days of receiving a declaration, the Ethics Officer enters the employee's post, the kind of declaration and the date of receipt.
Within 3 working days of a decision, the Ethics Officer adds the decision and its measures.
The Ethics Officer keeps each entry for 5 years after the employment of the employee ends, and then deletes it.
Only the Ethics Officer and the Chair of the Governing Board read the register.

## Section 6. Gifts

Section 4 of the Gifts and Hospitality Rule of the Organisation sets the gift threshold at 150 euros for one gift.
A gift from a counterparty with a value of 150 euros or less is not an interest and is not declared under this part.
A gift from a counterparty with a value of more than 150 euros is an interest that begins and ends on the day of receipt, and the employee files an event declaration for it under Section 3 of this part.
The employee estimates the value of the gift at its retail price on the day of receipt.
An employee who cannot establish the retail price treats the gift as worth more than 150 euros.

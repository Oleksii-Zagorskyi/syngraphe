# Standard Operating Procedure: Calibration of Laboratory Balances

## 1. Purpose

This procedure sets how the Organisation calibrates its laboratory balances, how often, and how each calibration is recorded.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to every balance entered in the equipment register of the Organisation.

## 3. Terms

Calibration means comparing the indication of a balance with the certified mass of reference weights, without changing any setting of the balance.
An analytical balance is a balance with a readability of 0.1 mg or finer.
A precision balance is a balance with a readability coarser than 0.1 mg.
The due date is the last calendar day on which the next calibration of a balance may be performed.
A working day is a day on which the laboratory is open.

## 4. Roles

The Technician performs every calibration and writes its record.
The Laboratory Manager keeps the equipment register, the calibration log and the reference weights.
The Laboratory Manager records in the equipment register, for each balance, its class under Section 3 and its low, middle and high calibration loads.
The Laboratory Manager sends each reference weight for recertification before its certificate expires.
The Quality Manager decides on results affected by a failed calibration under Section 8 and answers to the Director for those decisions.

## 5. Calibration Interval

The Technician calibrates each analytical balance no later than 30 calendar days after the date of its last passed calibration.
The Technician calibrates each precision balance no later than 90 calendar days after the date of its last passed calibration.
The Technician calibrates a new balance before its first use.
Only the Laboratory Manager orders the move, adjustment or repair of a balance, and the Laboratory Manager attaches an "Out of service" label to the balance when giving the order.
After a move, adjustment or repair, the Technician calibrates the balance before it returns to use.
The Analyst, a role that weighs on balances, does not use a balance that has no calibration label, whose due date has passed, or which carries an "Out of service" label.

## 6. Tolerance

The error at a load is the indication of the balance minus the certified mass of the reference weight.
The tolerance for an analytical balance is 0.3 mg at each load.
The tolerance for a precision balance is 0.03 g at each load.
A calibration is passed when the absolute value of the error is equal to or less than the tolerance at all three loads.
A calibration is failed when the absolute value of the error is greater than the tolerance at one or more loads.

## 7. Calibration Steps

The Technician performs the following steps in the order listed.

- The Technician confirms that the certificate of each reference weight to be used is valid on the day of calibration.
- The Technician confirms that the balance has been switched on for at least 60 minutes.
- The Technician levels the balance and cleans the weighing pan.
- The Technician sets the balance to zero with an empty pan.
- The Technician places the low load, then the middle load, then the high load on the pan, each alone, and notes the stable indication for each.
- The Technician calculates the error at each load and compares it with the tolerance in Section 6.
- For an analytical balance, the Technician accepts an error of up to 0.5 mg at the middle load.
- After a passed calibration, the Technician attaches a calibration label showing the calibration date and the due date, and removes any "Out of service" label.
- After a failed calibration, the Technician follows Section 8 instead of attaching a calibration label.
- The Technician completes the record under Section 9 on the day of calibration.

If a certificate is not valid, the Technician stops and informs the Laboratory Manager on the same working day.
A stopped calibration is neither passed nor failed, needs no record, and does not change the due date.
The Technician starts again from the first step once the Laboratory Manager provides reference weights with valid certificates.

## 8. Failed Calibration

After each failed calibration, the Technician removes the calibration label, attaches an "Out of service" label to the balance at once, and informs the Laboratory Manager on the same working day.
If the balance has a built-in calibration function, the Technician first runs that calibration, which resets the sensitivity of the balance, and then repeats the steps of Section 7.
The Laboratory Manager then either orders adjustment or repair of the balance under Section 5 or removes it from the equipment register.
The Laboratory Manager informs the Quality Manager of the failed calibration within 2 working days.
The Quality Manager decides which results weighed on the balance since its last passed calibration must be repeated, and writes the decision and its reasons in the record of the failed calibration.
The Laboratory Manager assigns each repeat weighing to an Analyst.

## 9. Calibration Record

The Technician writes one record in the calibration log for each passed and each failed calibration.
The record contains the following items.

- The identification number and class of the balance.
- The date of calibration.
- The identification number and certified mass of each reference weight.
- The indication and the error at each of the three loads.
- The tolerance applied and the result, passed or failed.
- The new due date, for a passed calibration only.
- The signature of the Technician.

The Laboratory Manager checks and signs each record within 5 working days of the calibration.
The Laboratory Manager keeps each record for 6 years from the date of calibration.
